## Background
What the Day-to-Day Actually Looks Like

This role lives in the gap between a sales director who has a health system opportunity and a proposal that needs to go out the door. Your day-to-day would look something like:

A Senior Director flags a pursuit, maybe a health system needs Epic optimization staffing or a cybersecurity program buildout. You jump in, lead technical discovery calls with the client to understand scope, translate that into a resource model and delivery framework, build the pricing with defensible assumptions, and produce the proposal content or SOW within their SLA windows (5 days for solution briefs, 10 for full proposals).

In parallel you'd be standardizing the templates and QA processes so the next proposal is faster and tighter than the last one. And periodically you'd be in front of CIOs and CTOs doing whiteboard sessions or participating in executive briefings to reinforce technical credibility before a deal closes.

It's deadline-driven, parallel-tracked work. You'd likely have multiple pursuits in flight at once at different stages.

Which Aya Products and Services This Touches

Based on the JD and what Aya actually sells, this role would support:

- HIT staffing engagements, their core offering for health system IT roles on a contingent or project basis
- Managed services or consulting-adjacent delivery models for EHR optimization, which is likely their highest volume HIT pursuit type
- Their VMS platform indirectly, since health system clients using Aya's vendor management software are also prospects for HIT staffing
- Cybersecurity, data and analytics, and clinical application support service lines, which sound like growth areas they're trying to build more structured pursuit capability around

You probably wouldn't be deep in the software platform itself. This role is on the services and staffing side, using the platform as a credibility and delivery enabler rather than as the product you're architecting around.

## Questions and Interview Notes
Questions to Expect

From a TA specialist the questions will be behavioral and fit-oriented, not deeply technical. Expect:

- Around short tenure in current SA role/Why are you interested in leaving your current role:

I joined into a newly established solutions architecture function and had the opportunity to take on a significant amount of responsibility early, including leading vendor evaluations and building out documentation standards.

The role has been a strong experience, but it’s primarily internally focused. What I’m looking for next is the opportunity to move into a pre-sales environment where I can apply the same skillset across multiple organizations and be more directly tied to solution strategy and outcomes like win rates and delivery models.

This role aligns closely with that direction.

- Walk me through your background and how it led you here:

I started in direct patient care as a critical care float tech at Saint Francis, which gave me a grounded understanding of how clinical environments operate under pressure. While doing that, I completed a biomedical engineering degree, which gave me the technical foundation to understand medical systems beyond a typical IT lens.

I then moved into Brainlab supporting image-guided neurosurgery in the OR, where I worked directly with surgeons across more than 100 cases. That experience gave me a strong appreciation for how technology decisions impact real clinical workflows.

From there, I transitioned into healthcare IT consulting with InteleShare, where I progressed to senior consultant. I worked across interoperability and imaging ecosystems using standards like FHIR, HL7, DICOM, OAuth, and SAML, while also building workflow diagrams and partnering with product and engineering teams. That’s where I started developing a pre-sales mindset.

In my current role at St Charles, I joined a newly formed solutions architecture function and helped build it from the ground up. I’ve led vendor evaluations, developed architecture standards, and worked directly with executive stakeholders on strategic initiatives.

This role is a natural next step into a more formal pre-sales capacity, where I can apply that experience to shaping solutions earlier and influencing outcomes across multiple organizations.

- Describe a time you supported a complex pre-sales or proposal effort

I worked on a provider compensation initiative where HR had already initiated an RFP without technical alignment. I stepped in to lead technical discovery across stakeholders, including finance, HR, and IT.

I evaluated multiple SaaS vendors, developed current and future state architecture, and identified integration requirements across systems like Epic and Workday. I also documented risks and scope gaps that weren’t addressed in the original RFP.

The key outcome was that leadership had a much clearer understanding of total solution impact before making a decision.

That experience translates directly to pre-sales because I was effectively evaluating the quality of vendor proposals and identifying what differentiated strong solutions from incomplete ones.

- How do you handle multiple high-priority deadlines at once

I prioritize based on business impact and decision dependency. If something is blocking an executive decision or downstream work, it gets immediate focus.

I also standardize as much as possible. For example, I’ve built reusable templates for architecture documentation and evaluations, which reduces turnaround time significantly.

For parallel workstreams, I break deliverables into smaller components and create clear checkpoints so progress is visible and predictable. That prevents last-minute compression and maintains quality under tight timelines.

- Tell me about a time you presented a technical solution to an executive audience

When presenting to executives, I focus on translating technical details into business impact.

For example, during a presentation for a solution with our CIO, CMO and a VP, I presented a slide deck that ran through the what and why behind the review to ensure alignment, which vendors were being evaluated, key differences between the options, and which option I recommend we proceed with. The solution details were focused on risk, scalability, and operational impact rather than technical components. I used simple diagrams to show how systems interact and where failure points exist.

The goal is not to walk through every detail but to enable a confident decision. That means being concise, anticipating questions, and clearly stating a recommendation.

- What does good solution architecture documentation look like to you

Good documentation is clear, standardized, and decision-oriented.

At a minimum, it should include current state, future state, integration points, data flows, and identified risks. It should also clearly define assumptions and scope boundaries.

From a usability standpoint, it needs to be consumable by both technical and non-technical stakeholders, which means strong visuals and minimal unnecessary detail.

The goal is consistency so that documentation can be reused, compared, and scaled across initiatives.

- Why Aya specifically

Aya’s model is interesting because it sits at the intersection of healthcare IT and workforce solutions. Instead of just delivering technology, you’re designing and deploying the people and delivery models that make those initiatives successful.

The scale of the organization and the range of initiatives you support, from EHR optimization to data and cybersecurity, creates an opportunity to work across multiple enterprise environments rather than a single health system.

What stands out most is the emphasis on pre-sales solution quality and speed, which aligns with how I’ve been operating from the buyer side.
