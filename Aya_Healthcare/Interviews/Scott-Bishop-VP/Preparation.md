**A. “How do you approach solutioning in a pre-sales context?”**

What he wants

- Structured thinking
- Balance of speed vs accuracy
- Commercial awareness

Talk track

I start with focused discovery to understand the client’s true problem, not just the stated request. From there, I define a target outcome and work backwards into a delivery model that includes the right skillsets, level of effort, and dependencies.

I prioritize clarity over completeness early, then iterate as more information becomes available. At the same time, I’m identifying risks, assumptions, and scope boundaries so the solution is both competitive and executable.

**B. “How would you build a staffing / delivery model?”**

Likely your biggest gap area → prepare this well

Talk track

I break it into three layers.

First is scope decomposition. What capabilities are required across clinical, technical, and operational domains.

Second is role mapping. Aligning each capability to specific roles, skill levels, and expected effort.

Third is phasing. Structuring the work into phases like assessment, design, implementation, and optimization, which helps align staffing levels over time.

From there, I sanity check against cost, timeline, and risk to ensure it’s realistic and defensible.

**“How do you handle ambiguity or incomplete requirements?”**

Talk track

That’s the default state in most engagements. I move forward by making assumptions explicit and validating them quickly.

I’d rather provide a structured initial solution with clearly defined assumptions than wait for perfect information. That allows the conversation to progress and gives stakeholders something concrete to react to.
