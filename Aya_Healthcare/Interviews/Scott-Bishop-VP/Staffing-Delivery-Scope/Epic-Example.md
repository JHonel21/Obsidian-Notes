Mock Scenario

You’re supporting a late-stage pursuit.

A mid-sized health system (~300 providers, multi-site) is struggling with:

- Provider burnout tied to Epic inefficiencies
- Poor reporting and limited visibility into operational metrics
- Fragmented workflows across ambulatory clinics

They’ve asked Aya to propose a 90-day engagement to:

- Assess and improve clinical workflows
- Optimize Epic configuration
- Improve reporting capabilities

They want:

- Fast impact
- Minimal disruption
- Clear ROI

Your Task (Respond as if in interview)

Walk me through:

1. How you would approach discovery (what you need to know and how fast)

2. How you would break this into workstreams

3. Your initial staffing model (roles, levels, rough structure)

4. Your delivery framework (phases and how work gets executed)

5. Key assumptions and risks you would call out in the proposal

Constraints (Important)

- You do NOT have perfect information
- You have 2–3 days to turn this into a proposal
- Sales is pushing for speed
- Client is cost-sensitive

1. Discovery Approach

- I’d start with targeted discovery focused on speed and clarity rather than completeness.
- In the first 24–48 hours, I’d align on three things:
- First, where the biggest sources of provider friction actually are. That usually means specific workflows like documentation, order entry, or in-basket management, not just “Epic inefficiency” broadly.
- Second, I’d assess current reporting capabilities. Whether the issue is data availability, report design, or adoption.
- Third, I’d identify constraints. Things like internal Epic governance, availability of SMEs, and any ongoing initiatives that could impact this work.
- The goal is to define a clear problem statement and boundaries quickly so we can move into solutioning without waiting for perfect information.

2. Workstream Breakdown

I’d break this into three primary workstreams:

Clinical Workflow Optimization

Focused on evaluating current-state workflows, identifying inefficiencies, and defining improved future-state workflows.

Epic Configuration Optimization

Translating those workflow improvements into system-level changes, including build adjustments, preference list optimization, and template standardization.

Reporting and Analytics

Improving visibility into operational metrics by assessing current reports, defining key performance indicators, and delivering actionable dashboards.

I’d also layer in light program management across all three to ensure coordination and timeline control.

3. Staffing Model

I’d structure staffing to align with those workstreams and phase the effort.

At a high level:

- 1 Program Manager to coordinate across workstreams and maintain executive visibility
- 2 Senior Epic Analysts to lead workflow optimization and configuration changes
- 1 Reporting/Analytics resource to focus on data and dashboard development
- Optional SME overlay for targeted clinical validation, depending on client availability

From a phasing perspective:

- Heavier senior presence in the first few weeks for assessment and design
- More execution-focused effort in the middle phase
- Tapering toward optimization and knowledge transfer at the end

The goal is to stay lean but ensure enough senior oversight to move quickly and avoid rework.

4. Delivery Framework

I’d structure this as a 90-day phased engagement:

Phase 1: Assessment (Weeks 1–3)

Rapid workflow evaluation, stakeholder interviews, and current-state analysis across workflows and reporting.

Phase 2: Design (Weeks 3–5)

Define future-state workflows, align on Epic changes, and prioritize high-impact improvements.

Phase 3: Implementation (Weeks 5–10)

Execute configuration updates, implement workflow changes, and develop reporting assets.

Phase 4: Optimization (Weeks 10–12)

Validate outcomes, refine configurations, and ensure adoption with end users.

Throughout, I’d include weekly governance with stakeholders and clear progress tracking tied to defined metrics.

5. Assumptions and Risks

I’d explicitly call out a few key assumptions:

- Timely access to Epic environments and reporting tools
- Availability of clinical stakeholders for workflow validation
- No major competing initiatives that would limit engagement

Key risks would include:

- Resistance to workflow changes impacting adoption
- Underestimated reporting complexity depending on data structure
- Timeline compression if access or decisions are delayed

I’d surface these early and tie them directly to timeline and outcome risk so expectations are clear.

6. Close (This Is What Makes It Strong)

- Overall, the goal is to deliver a focused, high-impact engagement that balances speed and quality.
- I’d intentionally keep the model lean and prioritize the highest-value improvements rather than trying to solve everything in 90 days, which keeps the proposal both competitive and achievable.

Pushback #1: “This feels a little heavy for a 90-day engagement. How would you simplify or reduce cost?”

What he’s testing

- Can you right-size without losing credibility
- Do you understand margin + competitiveness

Strong response

- That’s fair. If cost is a concern, I’d simplify in two ways.
- First, I’d narrow scope to the highest-impact workflows rather than broad coverage. For example, focusing on 2–3 priority areas like in-basket and documentation instead of all ambulatory workflows.
- Second, I’d rebalance the staffing model by reducing senior coverage after the initial assessment phase and introducing more mid-level execution resources where appropriate.
- The goal would be to preserve outcome quality while lowering overall cost by focusing effort where it drives the most value.

Pushback #2: “How do you ensure this actually drives ROI in 90 days?”

What he’s testing

- Outcome orientation
- Ability to tie work to measurable value

Strong response

- I’d define success metrics upfront and align them directly to the targeted workflows.
- For example, reduction in time spent in documentation, improvement in in-basket turnaround time, or increased adoption of optimized templates.
- I’d also prioritize quick wins early in the engagement so there’s visible progress within the first 30–45 days.
- That combination of defined metrics and early impact helps demonstrate ROI within the 90-day window.

Pushback #3: “What if the client doesn’t have strong internal engagement?”

What he’s testing

- Real-world delivery awareness
- Risk mitigation

Strong response

- That’s one of the biggest risks in this type of engagement.
- I’d address it in two ways. First, I’d make stakeholder participation an explicit assumption in the proposal, including expected time commitments.
- Second, I’d structure governance with regular checkpoints so lack of engagement becomes visible quickly rather than impacting outcomes later.
- If needed, we could also adjust by taking on more of the analysis work independently, but that would need to be reflected in scope and cost.

Pushback #4: “Why wouldn’t we just staff this with cheaper resources?”

What he’s testing

- Your ability to defend staffing strategy

Strong response

- You could reduce cost with more junior resources, but in a compressed 90-day engagement, that typically introduces more risk and slows decision-making.
- My approach is to use senior resources early to accelerate assessment and design, which reduces rework and keeps the timeline intact.
- From there, you can shift to more cost-effective resources for execution where appropriate.
- It’s about balancing speed, quality, and cost rather than optimizing for just one.
