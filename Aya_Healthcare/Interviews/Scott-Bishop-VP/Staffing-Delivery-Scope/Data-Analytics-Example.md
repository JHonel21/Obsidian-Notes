Mock Scenario #3: Data & Analytics Modernization

A multi-hospital health system is struggling with:

- Fragmented data across Epic, finance, and operational systems
- Heavy reliance on manual reporting (Excel extracts, static reports)
- Limited trust in data and inconsistent definitions

They want Aya to propose a 120-day engagement to:

- Assess current data architecture and reporting landscape
- Improve data accessibility and reporting capabilities
- Establish a foundation for scalable analytics

Constraints:

- Cost-sensitive
- No appetite for a full data platform rebuild
- Need visible improvement within 90–120 days

VP-Calibrated Talk Track

1. Discovery Approach

- I’d focus discovery on understanding where data breaks down today and where it impacts decision-making most.
- In the first couple of days, I’d align on three areas.
- First, key reporting use cases. What decisions are being made and where current reporting is failing.
- Second, data flow and architecture at a high level, how data moves from source systems like Epic into reporting tools.
- Third, data governance and ownership, including how definitions are managed and where inconsistencies arise.
- The goal is to quickly identify the highest-impact gaps without getting pulled into a full platform redesign.

2. Workstreams

I’d break this into three focused workstreams:

Data & Architecture Assessment

Understanding current pipelines, data sources, and fragmentation points.

Reporting & Use Case Enablement

Prioritizing a small number of high-value reporting use cases and improving how those are delivered.

Data Governance & Standardization

Establishing consistent definitions, ownership, and basic governance processes to improve trust in the data.

Program management would sit across all three to maintain alignment and pacing.

3. Staffing Model

I’d keep the model lean and aligned to those workstreams:

- 1 Program Manager
- 1 Data Architect to lead assessment and target-state definition
- 1–2 Data Engineers to work on pipelines and data accessibility
- 1 BI / Reporting Analyst to focus on dashboards and end-user needs

From a phasing standpoint, the architect is more heavily involved early, while engineering and reporting ramp during implementation.

The goal is to balance strategic direction with hands-on execution.

4. Delivery Framework

I’d structure this as a phased engagement:

Phase 1: Assessment (Weeks 1–3)

Evaluate current architecture, reporting gaps, and priority use cases.

Phase 2: Design (Weeks 3–6)

Define a simplified target-state architecture and prioritize a small set of high-impact reporting improvements.

Phase 3: Implementation (Weeks 6–14)

Improve data pipelines where needed, develop dashboards, and enable access to cleaner, more reliable data.

Phase 4: Stabilization (Weeks 14–16)

Validate outputs, refine definitions, and ensure adoption.

Throughout, I’d keep governance light but consistent, with clear ownership and progress tracking.

5. Assumptions and Risks

Key assumptions:

- Access to source systems and existing reporting tools
- Availability of stakeholders to define and validate use cases
- No major platform migration required within this engagement

Key risks:

- Data quality issues that require more effort than expected
- Misalignment on definitions impacting trust in outputs
- Scope creep toward full platform redesign

I’d mitigate that by tightly prioritizing use cases and clearly defining scope boundaries upfront.

6. Close

I’d position this as a focused effort to improve data usability and trust in the short term, while laying the groundwork for more advanced analytics over time.

The goal is not to rebuild the data platform in 120 days, but to make meaningful improvements that stakeholders can see and use quickly.

Likely VP Pushback + Strong Responses

“Why not just implement a new data platform?”

That’s a longer-term solution, but it introduces cost, complexity, and time that don’t align with a 120-day engagement.

I’d focus first on improving what they already have and proving value, then use that as a foundation for any future platform decisions.

“How do you ensure adoption?”

By anchoring the work to specific use cases and involving stakeholders early in defining and validating outputs.

If the solution solves real problems they experience daily, adoption tends to follow.

“How do you keep this from expanding?”

By tightly prioritizing a small number of use cases and explicitly defining what is out of scope, especially anything related to full platform redesign.

That keeps the engagement focused and achievable.

“What differentiates this approach?”

The focus on delivering usable outcomes quickly rather than designing a future-state architecture that takes years to realize.

It’s a more pragmatic approach that aligns with organizations that need immediate improvement.

Why This Scenario Is Your Advantage

- Experience with enterprise systems (Epic, ServiceNow, etc.)
- Understanding of cross-system data flow
- Real-world reporting challenges from the health system side

Final Positioning Line (Use This)

“A lot of organizations don’t need a full rebuild immediately. They need better access to the data they already have and confidence in how it’s used. That’s where I’d focus in a 120-day engagement.”
