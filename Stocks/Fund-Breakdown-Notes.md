## Fund Details and Expenses

### Expense Ratio (Gross and Net: 0.45%)

- **What it means**: The annual fee charged to manage the fund, expressed as a percentage of your investment ($4.50 per $1,000 invested). Gross is the total cost, while Net is what investors actually pay after any temporary fee waivers.
    
- **Good vs. Bad**: **0.45% is good for an actively managed mid-cap fund**. While passive index funds can be found for under 0.10%, active managers frequently charge 0.75% or higher. Because the net and gross ratios match, there are no expiring fee waivers to worry about.
    

### Turnover Rate (97.00%)

- **What it means**: This measures how frequently the fund manager buys and sells securities within the portfolio over a year. A 97% rate means the manager replaces nearly the entire portfolio annually.
    
- **Good vs. Bad**: **97% is high**. High turnover signifies aggressive active management. The downside is that frequent trading generates higher transaction costs and can trigger short-term capital gains distributions, which increases tax drag if held in a taxable brokerage account. It matters less inside a tax-advantaged account like an IRA.
    

### Portfolio Net Assets ($153.32M)

- **What it means**: The total pool of capital managed within this specific share class.
    
- **Good vs. Bad**: **$153.32 million is relatively small** for a Fidelity mutual fund. While a smaller asset base gives the manager flexibility to buy smaller stocks without moving market prices, it also means the fund has less institutional scale. It is large enough to avoid liquidation risk, but it is not a massive flagship fund.
    

### 12 Month Low-High ($11.28 - $14.91)

- **What it means**: The lowest and highest NAV recorded over the past year.
    
- **Good vs. Bad**: This is a historical reference point. The fact that the current NAV ($15.72) sits above the 12-month high indicates the fund is currently trading at peak performance.
