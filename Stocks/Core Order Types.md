Market Order

Buy or sell immediately at the best available current price.

Characteristics
- Executes quickly
- Prioritizes execution over price
- Price can move before execution completes

Best for
- Large, highly liquid stocks
- Long-term investing
- When small price differences do not matter much

Example
You place a market order for 10 shares of Apple Inc. (AAPL) at market open. You get the next available asking price.

Risk
In volatile stocks, the actual execution price may differ significantly from what you expected. This is called slippage.

  

Limit Order

You set the maximum price you are willing to pay when buying, or minimum price when selling.

Characteristics

- Gives price control
- May not execute if price never reaches your limit

Best for

- Volatile stocks
- Precision entry points
- Thinly traded stocks

Example

You place a limit order to buy NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) at $115. The order only executes if shares become available at $115 or lower.

  

Stop Order (Stop-Loss)

Becomes a market order once a trigger price is hit.

Characteristics

- Often used to limit downside
- Trigger activates automatically

Example

You own a stock at $50 and place a stop order at $45. If the stock drops to $45, the system submits a market sell order.

Risk

In fast-moving markets, execution may occur well below the stop price.

  

Stop-Limit Order

Combines a stop trigger with a limit price.

Characteristics

- More price control than stop orders
- May fail to execute during rapid moves

Example

- Stop price: $45
- Limit price: $44

If triggered, the stock only sells at $44 or better.

  

Time-Based Order Instructions

These determine how long the order remains active.

Day Order

Expires at market close if unfilled.

Most brokerage orders default to this.

  

Good-Til-Canceled (GTC)

Stays active until:

- executed
- canceled manually
- broker expiration period reached

Useful for long-term target prices.

  

Immediate-Or-Cancel (IOC)

Executes immediately for whatever shares are available. Remaining shares cancel.

Used more by active traders and institutions.

  

Fill-Or-Kill (FOK)

Entire order must execute immediately or cancel entirely.

Common for large institutional trades.

  

Purchase Strategies

These are broader investing approaches rather than order mechanics.

Lump Sum Investing

Invest all available capital at once.

Best when

- Long investment horizon
- Strong market conviction

Historically, lump sum investing often outperforms dollar-cost averaging over long periods because markets trend upward over time.

  

Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)

Invest fixed amounts on a schedule regardless of price.

Characteristics

- Reduces emotional investing
- Smooths volatility
- Common in retirement investing

Example

Buying $500 of Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) every two weeks.

  

Fractional Share Purchasing

Buy portions of a stock instead of whole shares.

Example

You buy $25 worth of Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) instead of an entire share.

Common with

- New investors
- Expensive stocks
- Automated investing

  

Advanced Trading Methods

Margin Buying

Borrow money from your broker to buy more stock.

Characteristics

- Increases upside potential
- Increases downside risk
- Interest is charged

Risk

Margin calls can force liquidation during downturns.

  

Options-Based Stock Acquisition

Cash-Secured Put

You sell a put option and potentially acquire shares at a lower effective price.

Covered Call Entry/Exit

Generate income while holding shares.

These are more advanced derivatives strategies.

  

Ways to Access Stocks

Individual Stocks

Direct ownership of companies.

Examples:

- Microsoft Corporation (MSFT)
- Constellation Energy Corporation (CEG)

  

ETFs

Funds that trade like stocks.

Advantages

- Diversification
- Lower risk than single stocks
- Easy exposure to sectors or indexes

Examples:

- SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY)
- Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ)

  

Mutual Funds

Purchased directly through fund companies or brokerages.

Differences from ETFs

- Priced once daily after market close
- Not traded intraday
