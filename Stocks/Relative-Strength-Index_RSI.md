RSI is displayed on a scale from 0 to 100 and is commonly used to identify whether a stock may be:

- Overbought → potentially due for a pullback
- Oversold → potentially due for a rebound

The standard interpretation is:

- RSI above 70  
    Often considered overbought
- RSI below 30  
    Often considered oversold

The default calculation usually uses the past 14 trading periods.

The formula conceptually compares:

- Average gains over a period  
    vs.
- Average losses over a period

The RSI itself is:

RSI = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + RS}

Where:

RS = \frac{\text{Average Gain}}{\text{Average Loss}}

Practical Example

If a stock has:

- RSI = 78  
    Traders may think momentum is overheated.
- RSI = 24  
    Traders may think selling pressure is exhausted.

Important Limitation

RSI is a momentum indicator, not a prediction tool.

A stock can stay:

- overbought for a long time in a strong uptrend
- oversold for a long time in a strong downtrend

Because of that, traders usually combine RSI with:

- support/resistance
- moving averages
- volume
- MACD
- trend analysis

Common RSI Strategies

- RSI bounce from 30 → possible entry signal
- RSI rejection near 70 → possible exit/take-profit signal
- Bullish divergence → price makes lower lows while RSI makes higher lows
- Bearish divergence → price makes higher highs while RSI makes lower highs
